THE PREGNANCY JOURNEY

Nine months, one step at a time.

A gentle overview of what often happens at each stage. Every pregnancy is different, so use this as a guide for the conversation, not as a checklist to measure yourself against.

WEEKS 1 TO 12

First trimester

The very beginning. Much is happening inside, even if little shows outside.

What often happens
  • Nausea, tiredness and tender breasts
  • Mood changes
  • The baby's organs begin to form
Often done around now
  • Confirming the pregnancy and a first scan
  • First blood tests
  • Starting vitamins as advised
Good to ask
  • What to eat, and which medicines are safe
  • When to call if something worries you
WEEKS 13 TO 27

Second trimester

Many women feel better in this stretch, with more energy and a growing bump.

What often happens
  • Nausea often eases
  • You may feel your baby's first movements
  • Back ache or leg cramps can begin
Often done around now
  • A detailed anomaly scan around the middle of this stage
  • Routine blood tests and a sugar test
  • Vaccines as advised
Good to ask
  • Safe exercise and diet
  • What baby movements to expect
WEEKS 28 TO 40

Third trimester

The home stretch. Check-ups become more frequent as the birth gets closer.

What often happens
  • Swelling, back ache and trouble sleeping
  • Tightening in the tummy now and then
  • Your baby settles into position
Often done around now
  • Growth scans if advised
  • Talking about where and how you'll deliver
  • Packing your hospital bag
Good to ask
  • Signs that labour is starting
  • When to come in, and who to call
THE FIRST WEEKS

After the birth

A tender time of recovery, feeding and very little sleep. Looking after you matters too.

What often happens
  • Tiredness and gradual healing
  • Feeding takes practice
  • Mood can swing, and that is common
Often done around now
  • A check-up for you in the first weeks
  • Baby's own check-ups and vaccines
Good to ask
  • Feeding, rest and recovery
  • How you're feeling, in yourself

General information only. Dr. Sandesh will guide you on what is right for your own pregnancy.

QUESTIONS PEOPLE ASK

Things you may be wondering.

No question is too small. If yours isn't here, you can always ask.

What happens on a video consultation?
  1. Pick a time that suits you, between 5 and 8 PM, Monday to Saturday.
  2. Join the call from your phone, from wherever you are comfortable.
  3. Leave with a clear next step, explained in plain words.
What should I keep ready?
  • The date your last period started
  • Any scan or test reports you have
  • A list of medicines or vitamins you take
  • Your questions, written down so you don't forget
Why are video consultations in the evening?

Dr. Sandesh works at the hospital from 9 AM to 4 PM, so video consultations are kept for the evening, from 5 to 8 PM, Monday to Saturday.

Can I see Dr. Sandesh in person?

Dr. Sandesh also sees patients in the OPD at Siddaganga Hospital, Tumakuru, on Thursdays. For OPD details, please check with the hospital.

Will I need to visit in person?

Many concerns can be talked through on a video call. If something needs an in-person examination or tests, the doctor will advise you on the next step.

How do I choose a time, and what does it cost?

Choose a date and an available time in the consultation section. It is listed as a 15-minute consultation for ₹500. No payment is taken on the form, and the clinic confirms your appointment with you.

How often should I have check-ups in pregnancy?

A usual pattern is about once a month in the first six months, every two weeks from about 28 weeks, and weekly in the last month. Your doctor will set the exact pattern for you.

Is it okay to ask small or silly questions?

Yes, always. Small questions are often the ones that bring the most peace of mind.

Who sees the details I share?

What you share in the check-in or the booking form is used only so the clinic can get back to you. It is meant to be seen by Dr. Sandesh's team, and not by other visitors.

When should I not wait?

Heavy bleeding, severe pain, fluid leaking, a baby moving much less than usual, a severe headache with blurred vision, fits, fainting, chest pain or trouble breathing need urgent in-person care. Please go to the nearest hospital or call your doctor straight away.
